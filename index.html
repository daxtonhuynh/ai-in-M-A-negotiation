01 / Business context

A deal is more than
the final price.

A buyer wants to capture value. A seller wants a stronger price. Both face limited room to concede, a finite negotiation window and the cost of delay. Each offer changes the choices that follow.

This project turns those tensions into an explicit decision model, then asks which search methods can navigate that model efficiently—and what is lost when decisions use a shorter planning horizon.

THE BUSINESS CHALLENGE

Today's offer shapes tomorrow's options.

A seemingly attractive price can consume concession capacity or invite an unfavorable counteroffer. Evaluating one move in isolation misses the sequence.

Decision depth has a computational cost.

Every additional legal offer expands the decision tree. A complete search provides a benchmark, but spends effort on branches that may never affect the best decision.

THE ANALYTICAL RESPONSE

Make the commercial rules explicit.

Encode reservation values, synergy, concession budgets, delay cost and a negotiation deadline. Compare strategies within the same set of legal actions.

Separate efficiency from decision quality.

Use Minimax as the exact baseline, Alpha–Beta to remove unnecessary search, and depth-limited heuristics to examine the trade-off between computation and outcomes.

02 / Solution design

From business rules to testable decisions.

The buyer maximizes a relative payoff; the seller minimizes it. At each turn, the model considers legal offers, acceptance or exit. Both parties' information is visible, making the experiment controlled and the reasoning traceable.

01 — FRAME THE DEAL

Define room to negotiate.

Buyer reservation value plus synergy sets the upper valuation. Seller reservation value sets the lower bound. Concession budgets and delay costs constrain movement.

02 — SEARCH THE CHOICES

Evaluate the next move.

Full search establishes the benchmark. Alpha–Beta prunes irrelevant branches. Heuristics estimate positions when search stops before the negotiation ends.

03 — COMPARE THE EVIDENCE

Measure effort and outcomes.

Record node visits, deal completion, relative utility and deviations from the exact baseline across buyer-advantaged, balanced and seller-advantaged cases.

03 / Measured results

The same exact value. A smaller search.

84.6%

Less exploration.
No loss of exact root value.

Ordered Alpha–Beta visits about 789 nodes on average, compared with 5,127 for Minimax. It preserves the baseline root values across all nine scenarios while skipping branches that cannot improve the decision.

The business relevance: a more efficient evaluation engine creates room to explore additional scenarios. This is demonstrated computational efficiency, not a measured saving in transaction costs.

04 / What changed

Three lessons for decision design.

01

Optimize the evaluation process before simplifying the decision.

Exact pruning removed substantial search effort while preserving root values. Within this model, efficiency did not require accepting a lower-quality evaluation.

02

Deal completion alone is an incomplete success metric.

Depth-2 methods reached agreement in every scenario, but their outcomes differed more from the exact baseline. Completion, utility and computational effort need to be read together.

03

More elaborate heuristics need stronger evidence.

Adding delay and negotiation-pressure terms did not improve aggregate utility or deal completion over the simpler heuristic at the same depth. Complexity must earn its place through validation.

05 / Evidence & boundaries

Useful results.
Clear limits.

The outcome is an inspectable decision model and a reproducible comparison—not a claim that AI has improved a real merger. The next step is to test richer negotiation settings before drawing operational conclusions.

What makes the comparison reproducible?

The repository includes scenario definitions, Python implementations, raw and summary CSV outputs, plotting scripts and automated tests. Tests cover exact-search value equivalence, legal actions and model constraints. The full report explains the scoring rules, experimental design and interpretation.

What assumptions limit real-world use?

The model is deterministic, fully observable, finite-horizon and relative-zero-sum. It excludes private valuations, uncertainty, multi-issue bargaining, financing and integration risk. Nine synthetic scenarios provide controlled comparisons, not empirical evidence about real M&A performance. Runtime measurements are illustrative single-run observations.

What would strengthen the next iteration?

Expand the scenario grid, compare methods against different opponents, test sensitivity to concession and delay assumptions, and introduce uncertainty or private information. Evaluate decision quality and robustness before treating the model as an operational decision-support tool.
